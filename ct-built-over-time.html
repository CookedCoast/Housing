Connecticut Built Over Time

Every dot is a parcel, placed on the map in the year its main building was built. Press play and watch towns grow, from colonial village centers to postwar subdivisions to what's going up today.

Where the data comes from

The State of Connecticut's statewide CAMA and Parcel layer, compiled by the Connecticut GIS Office from municipal assessor records collected through the Councils of Government (C.G.S. §4d-90 to 92 and §7-100l).

  • Year built is the assessor's "Actual Year Built" (AYB) field.
  • Home type is grouped from each town's land-use description, which is worded differently from town to town.
  • Parcel locations are the center of each parcel. Details load from the state service when you click a dot.

How to use it

  1. Pick a place. Choose a town or one of the nine planning regions and the map loads it. Use + Add another… to put several towns or regions on the map together, and the × on each tag to remove one.
  2. Move through time. Press Play, or drag the slider or the bar chart along the bottom to jump to any year. New buildings flash orange, then settle into their decade color. Shaded bands on the timeline mark key eras in Connecticut housing, orange markers flag turning points, and a caption explains what was happening.
  3. Focus on one decade or era. Click a decade or era in the legend, an era name on the timeline, or the era caption, and the map shows only what was built then and pauses. Press Play span to watch just that period, or Show all to go back.
  4. Look closer. Click any dot for its address, land use and living area.
  5. See it from the air. Choose Aerials for historical aerial photos from 1934 to 2023, then turn on Swipe to compare and drag the divider to compare two years. Town and planning-region outlines can be turned on or off under Boundaries.
  6. Compare home types. The Home types panel tracks single family, multi-family, condos and more. Switch to Share to see how the mix changes.

Trends to look for

  • Historic cores. Clusters of the oldest decade colors mark village centers, mill towns and waterfronts built before 1900.
  • The postwar boom. From the late 1940s to the 1970s, new dots spread out from town centers along new roads.
  • A changing housing mix. In Share view, watch 2–4 family houses shrink as a share of the total while condos and townhouses grow from the 1970s on.
  • Where building is happening now. The newest decade colors show the 2010s and 2020s. See whether growth is infill near centers or new land at the edges.
  • Town vs. town. Load two towns or regions to compare when they grew and what kind of housing they built.

Keep in mind: the data only describes buildings standing today. Demolished buildings never appear, a teardown shows up as new construction, and assessors often round old build years, which causes spikes at years like 1900 and 1950.
