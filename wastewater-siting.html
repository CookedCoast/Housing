Ranks Connecticut parcels for sewer access or septic suitability from public GIS data: SSURGO soils, water table and bedrock, wetland soils and NDDB areas, DPH/DEEP offsets, and the Coastal Boundary. It's a desktop screen to use before deep tests and percolation tests, and it doesn't replace them.

How each criterion is scored, and the data behind it
CriterionData sourceRule used here
Sewer accessCT DEEP Connected Sewer Service Areas (2021)Parcel centroid inside a "Connected" area counts as sewer available. Capacity and an allocation still need to be confirmed with the local WPCA.
Soil / permeabilityNRCS SSURGO via Soil Data AccessHalf the score is the NRCS "ENG – Septic Tank Absorption Fields" rating (not limited 100, somewhat limited 65, very limited 20). The other half is the slowest saturated hydraulic conductivity (Ksat) between 25 and 152 cm. Ksat of 100 µm/s or more is treated as too fast: it caps the score and triggers 24 in of groundwater separation.
GroundwaterSSURGO minimum annual depth to water tableMargin = water-table depth − leaching bottom − required separation (18 in, or 24 in for 2,000 gpd and up, coastal, or very fast soils). No water table within 200 cm scores full.
BedrockSSURGO minimum depth to bedrockMargin = rock depth − leaching bottom − 48 in. A water table under 12 in or rock under 24 in below grade marks the site not feasible.
Wetlands / NDDBSSURGO drainage class and flooding; CT DEEP NDDB AreasPoorly drained, very poorly drained and floodplain map units count as wetland soils (the soil-based CT definition). Score = upland share of the parcel. An NDDB overlap subtracts 35 points and flags an NDDB review request.
DEEP / DPH offsetsParcel geometry, wetland soilsUsable area = the parcel shrunk by the lot-line setback, minus wetland soils buffered by the wetland offset. It is compared with the footprint needed for the design flow. Below 1.0× is not feasible.
Coastal zoneCT DEEP Coastal BoundaryInside the boundary: 45 points, CAM consistency review, and 24 in groundwater separation.

Review paths follow the design-flow thresholds: under 2,000 gpd, local director of health; 2,000–7,500 gpd, DPH plan approval; over 7,500 gpd on one site, a DEEP subsurface discharge permit. Vertical separations follow the DPH Technical Standards: the bottom of the leaching system at least 18 in above maximum groundwater (24 in for large systems, fast perc or tidal areas) and 48 in above ledge. Horizontal offsets that GIS can't see are not checked: wells (75 ft and up), open watercourses (50 ft), foundations, drains and slopes.

Bring your own attributes (no live lookup needed)

If your GIS has already overlaid the layers, include these properties (GeoJSON) or columns (CSV with lat, lon). Any value you supply is used as is. Missing values are fetched live when possible.

name, sewer (Y/N), septic_rating (Not limited | Somewhat limited | Very limited),
ksat_min, ksat_max (µm/s), wt_in, rock_in (inches below grade; blank = deeper than 79 in),
wetland_pct (0–100), nddb (Y/N), coastal (Y/N), area_sf, usable_sf
